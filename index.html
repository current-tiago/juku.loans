When two business loans are cheaper at different rate types, a comparative advantage swap lets both get the loan they wanted for less. You bring the client, we handle the swap.

Inside a swap

  1. Your client wants a fixed rate, but its best deal is floating at SOFR + 1.20%, so it borrows floating. The other business is the opposite: it wants floating but borrows fixed at 6.80%.

  2. Your client's floating loan costs more, so the other business pays it the difference through juku. Your client stays at 7.00% fixed.

    Your client's floating loan costs less, so it pays the other business the difference through juku. Your client still pays 7.00% fixed, and the other business's rate falls with SOFR.

  3. Your client ends up paying 7.00% fixed instead of the 7.20% it was quoted, and the other business saves the same 0.20% a year. You earn a commission from juku on the deal.

Interest rate swaps are regulated, so every deal follows the same rules the big banks work to.

  • Standard swap paperwork

    Every swap is documented on the ISDA Master Agreement, the contract banks around the world use for interest rate swaps. No home-made terms.

  • Only eligible businesses

    US law limits swaps like these to Eligible Contract Participants. For most businesses that means over $10 million in assets, or over $1 million in net worth and using the swap to manage its own loan. We check both sides before anything is signed.

  • Every party identified

    Each business has a Legal Entity Identifier, the global ID for companies in financial deals, so it's clear exactly who is on each side of a swap.

  • Reported to regulators

    Every swap is reported to a CFTC-registered swap data repository, as US rules require for all swaps, so regulators can see it.

  • Sanctions screened

    Both businesses are screened against the US Treasury's OFAC sanctions lists before a deal goes ahead.
