Inside a swap

  1. Lenders don't price every business the same way. Your client is offered a better deal on floating, and the other business on fixed. If each borrows its better deal instead of the rate it wants, together they pay less interest.

  2. So each takes the loan it's offered the better deal on: your client borrows floating, and the other business borrows fixed.

  3. Your client's floating loan costs more, so the other business pays it the difference through juku. Your client stays on its fixed rate.

    Your client's floating loan costs less, so it pays the other business the difference through juku. Your client stays on its fixed rate, and the other business's rate falls with SOFR.

  4. Each business ends up with the rate it wanted, for less than it was quoted. You earn a commission from juku on the deal.

Documentation1 of 5

ISDA Master Agreement

What it is

The ISDA Master Agreement, published by the International Swaps and Derivatives Association, is the standard contract for over-the-counter derivatives, including interest rate swaps. It sets out how payments are calculated and netted, what counts as a default, and how a swap is closed out if something goes wrong.

How we handle it

Every swap is documented on the ISDA Master Agreement and its Schedule, with a confirmation for each deal. The terms are the market standard rather than drafted from scratch, so both businesses and their advisers are reading a contract the market already knows.

Eligibility2 of 5

Eligible Contract Participant

What it is

Under the Commodity Exchange Act, swaps agreed away from an exchange are only open to Eligible Contract Participants. A business generally qualifies with total assets over $10 million, or with a net worth over $1 million where the swap manages risk in its own business, such as a loan.

How we handle it

Both businesses must qualify as Eligible Contract Participants, and we confirm this for each side before anything is signed. A business that doesn't qualify isn't offered a swap.

Identification3 of 5

Legal Entity Identifier

What it is

A Legal Entity Identifier is a 20-character code, defined by ISO 17442 and overseen by the Global Legal Entity Identifier Foundation, that identifies a company taking part in a financial transaction. US swap reporting rules require each party to a swap to be identified by its LEI.

How we handle it

Each business needs an active LEI before its swap can go ahead, so it is always clear exactly who is on each side. An LEI is renewed every year.

Reporting4 of 5

Swap data repository reporting

What it is

Under CFTC rules, every swap must be reported to a registered swap data repository when it is agreed, and again whenever it changes until it ends. Regulators use this data to oversee the swaps market.

How we handle it

Every swap arranged through juku is reported to a CFTC-registered swap data repository, and its record is kept up to date for the life of the deal.

Screening5 of 5

OFAC sanctions screening

What it is

The Office of Foreign Assets Control, part of the US Treasury, administers US economic sanctions. US persons are generally prohibited from dealing with anyone on its sanctions lists, including the Specially Designated Nationals list.

How we handle it

Both businesses are screened against OFAC's sanctions lists before a deal goes ahead.
